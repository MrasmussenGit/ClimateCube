How to read a scatterplot

Each blue dot is one indoor reading paired with the closest outdoor reading from the same time.
The orange line is the single straight line that best summarizes the overall cloud of dots. It is a guide, not a boundary or forecast.
Dots packed close to the line mean one value changes predictably with the other. Dots scattered widely mean other influences are important.
An upward line is a positive relationship: higher outdoor values tend to accompany higher indoor values. A downward line is negative.
A nearly flat line suggests little straight-line relationship. Curves, clusters, or separate groups may still reveal patterns a straight line misses.
Correlation is not causation. Time of day, HVAC, occupancy, sunlight, open windows, and weather can move both measurements together.

Indoor metric Outdoor metric Pearson r Measures how closely two values follow a straight-line relationship. +1 means they rise together perfectly, -1 means one falls as the other rises, and 0 means no linear relationship. Spearman ρ Compares the rank order of values rather than their exact size. It can find consistent increasing or decreasing relationships that are curved and is less sensitive to extreme values than Pearson correlation. Change Δr Correlation between one reading's change from the previous reading and the matching outdoor change. This asks whether the measurements move together, instead of merely being high or low at the same time of day. Best outdoor lead The tested delay where an outdoor change aligns most strongly with a later indoor change. “No delay” means the strongest match occurred in the same time block. Samples The number of aligned indoor/outdoor time blocks used for this relationship. More samples usually improve stability, but nearby readings are related to each other and are not fully independent evidence.

Time-of-day effects

A 24-hour sine/cosine model estimates how much variation follows a repeating daily cycle. R² is descriptive, not proof that time itself causes the change.

Indoor metric Daily-cycle R² The share of observed variation described by a smooth repeating 24-hour pattern. A value of 0.70 means about 70% follows that daily rhythm; it does not identify what causes the rhythm. Typical amplitude The estimated distance from the daily average to the cycle's peak. The modeled peak-to-low swing is approximately twice this value. Estimated peak time The local time when the fitted daily cycle reaches its highest value. This summarizes the overall period and may not match every individual day. Samples The number of indoor observations used to estimate the daily pattern. Missing periods and limited day coverage can make the estimated peak and amplitude less reliable.
